# Next-agent handoff: get from a valid file to a validated camera profile

## Start here

The target device is the base Samsung Galaxy S23 Snapdragon, SM-S911B. Deliver one look across main, ultrawide, 3× telephoto and front cameras, across regular photos, portraits, Night and supported video modes. Keep good highlight separation and shadow detail, without orange/red skin, blue bloom, noisy shadows, waxy texture or oversharpening. Prioritize one automatically useful profile, not a set of creative presets.

1. Target A is the BigKaka GCam mod **AGC9.2.14 V14.0**; the uploaded v15 Low Light file itself identifies 9.2 V14. Earlier work on AGC 9.6 v7 is historical, not the target. The internal nickname and changelog refer to older revisions; don't trust either as the active version.
2. Target B is **standalone AGC Camera 1.8.6**, not the GCam mod. Its native Profiles tab uses a v4 ZIP with root JSON. We confirmed that format against an actual export and the app's serializer. Basile gives the three native photo modes a neutral baseline; this is not a numeric GCam or video-settings conversion. Applying it replaces profile-managed overrides. Back up first. See [import instructions and evidence](standalone.md).
3. The generator and fixtures are usable without originals. The public baseline contains 451 retained, typed entries of 1,328 in the supplied map. Values are taken from source; the renderer does not normalize sensor-specific tuning. Public omissions include build fingerprints, history, private preferences, disabled fixed-address patches, paths, original labels/changelog, blank or library-default placeholders, and additional settings outside the reviewed capture subset. Some non-private functionality from a complete export may depend on omitted settings. Don't call this a full restore.
4. The source's JPEG quality is 97; RAW availability is on with RAW capture off. The four visible slot labels are main, wide, tele, selfie, but the data also has six per-slot groups. Camera ID sets are _sets_, not a lens-index map. Retain physical-lens differences and verify routing with a lens occlusion test. Do not promise 2× shortcut behavior using a lens zoom override. Do not infer the active profile solely from a numbering suffix.
5. The photo HDRNet override is present across all six p0 slots; saved video preferences include stabilization=1, HEVC=1 and 2160p/30 values. Other mode-specific fps keys differ; viewfinder values are not recorded fps. Desired video HDR on by default is **not verified/encoded**. There is no evidence that a frame-rate flag, HDRNet, or HEVC alone enables HDR10/HLG. HDR+ processing, Ultra HDR still gain maps, and HDR video are three separate acceptance tests.

## First controlled run

- Back up working settings; start on the known GCam build and record exact package/build locally. Test stock defaults and the generated preset; don't overwrite the only known-good backup. Reopen after import. Confirm main opens at 1×, front picker is sane, all expected lenses actually switch, modes load without crashes, no preset switching is needed.
- Use the same neutral card, skin and textured object in window/backlight, daylight, neutral indoor light and genuinely dim light. Fix framing and light, avoid clipping the chart, disable extra filters, label lens/app/mode explicitly. Keep original JPEGs locally. Judge skin hue, neutral balance, fine fabric, grain, temporal smearing and motion blur. Do not tune WB or raise all denoise values from four untagged SDR derivatives.
- Collect original, untranscoded videos as files/ZIP. If the two apps cannot be identified, call them A/B; obtain a labeled repeat, don't guess from appearance. Capture the same 10–20 s static, pan and walking sequence in each app, same focal length, HDR switch, stabilization, lighting, duration, fps and resolution. Repeat dim and daylight; reserve 30/60 and stabilized/unstabilized controls. Keep private originals out of Git. Compare aggregate results on-device and from the same player.
- Record separate rows for FHD30, FHD60, UHD30 and UHD60 for each accessible lens, with and without HDR; use unknown/unavailable explicitly. Note OIS/EIS indicators, crop, jumps, skew and stabilization quality. A phone's stock Samsung pipeline may expose combinations not available to a third-party app.
- Run `bun run video:cadence clip.mp4` locally. Compare signaled transfer/primaries, bit depth/pixel format, cadence, p95 interval and long gaps with the target interval (16.67 ms for 60 fps). Inspect frame content if metadata cadence is regular: duplicate images can carry unique timestamps. Distinguish capture drops, longer exposure blur and playback/display pacing. Compare straight-edge motion, not just crop. Results are evidence, not camera attribution.
- Validate HDR _video_ with a supported 10-bit pipeline plus HLG or PQ signaling, BT.2020 context where applicable, and actual highlight/playback behavior. HEVC Main 10 is insufficient on its own. Validate Ultra HDR _stills_ independently, with the [Android Ultra HDR image specification](https://developer.android.com/media/platform/hdr-image-format): inspect the secondary gain-map image and its association, not merely an XMP string. Confirm default persistence after a cold launch and lens/mode changes. Consult [Android HDR capture](https://developer.android.com/media/camera/camera2/hdr-video-capture) before promising combinations.
- Under repeated captures, check capture delay, heat, battery trend, crashes and available resolution/codec combinations. Report any real price paid for low noise or high dynamic range.

## Change policy and next device-validation steps

Compare a fresh export, then paired exports taken before/after switching exactly one feature (especially HDR video). Record target version, key _and type_, enum, scope, and what the recorded file actually contains. No guessed preference names, hardcoded executable patches, cross-version binary offsets, or forced devices/lenses. Unsupported combinations stay visible in the manifest/docs. Use the native UI for HDR where it works until evidence supports a serializable setting. A commit asserting a new mapping needs a stripped fixture, generator coverage, round-trip import on the right build, and device evidence. For standalone, re-export the generated profile after applying it; compare effective settings and test each new control with isolated before/after exports. Do not treat a successful ZIP import as proof that HDR video is configured.

For noise, first identify whether degradation is chroma noise, luma noise, motion or sharpening halos. Change one layer on one lens; repeat the same scene with a moving subject. Test the neighboring lenses/modes after every improvement. Keep the source's per-sensor noise profiles and exposure/frame-count differences unless measurements justify changes; equal sliders do not guarantee equal pictures. Do not disable stabilization to conceal dropped frames or increase aggressive denoise merely to hide texture.
